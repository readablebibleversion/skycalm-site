Fear of Flying Tips: How to Prepare in the Week Before Your Flight

A 6-minute read

For many nervous flyers, the week before the flight is harder than the flight itself. The worry builds quietly, and the mind keeps rehearsing what might go wrong. The good news is that you can use this week. A little preparation each day gives your nerves somewhere useful to go. Here's a simple plan.

Seven to five days before: learn what's normal

Fear grows in the gaps where you don't know what's happening. Filling those gaps is one of the most helpful things you can do.

  • Read up on turbulence. Knowing why it happens and why planes handle it makes bumps much less frightening.
  • Learn the sounds. The thud after take-off, the engines going quieter, the whir of the flaps. If you've heard them before, they won't surprise you.
  • Picture the flight in order. Boarding, taxi, take-off, cruise, descent, landing. Knowing the sequence makes it feel more manageable.

Keep it calm and factual. Avoid scrolling through dramatic videos and news stories. They're chosen for shock, not for how flying really is.

Five to four days before: sort the practical stuff

Every small thing you settle now is one less thing to worry about later.

  • Choose your seat. Over the wing is usually the smoothest. Pick aisle or window depending on whether you like to see out or like to get up easily.
  • Check your documents, bag rules and how you'll get to the airport.
  • Plan to arrive early. Rushing raises your stress before you've even boarded.
  • Tell your travel companion what helps you, and what doesn't. If you're flying alone, tell a friend you'll message them before take-off and after landing.

Every day this week: practise calm

Calming skills work best when they're familiar. If you practise when you're relaxed, they'll come more easily when you're anxious.

  • Breathe for five minutes a day. In through the nose for 4, out through the mouth for 6. A longer breath out helps calm your body.
  • Try 5-4-3-2-1 grounding. Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can feel, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell and 1 you can taste.
  • Imagine the hard moment. Close your eyes, picture a bumpy patch, and practise your breathing while you picture it. You're teaching your body a new response.

When the worry shows up during the week, that's fine. Notice it, name it ("there's the flight worry again"), and gently go back to what you were doing. You don't have to win an argument with it.

Three to two days before: pack a comfort kit

Put together a small bag for your seat with things that help you feel settled:

  • Headphones and something to listen to, downloaded in advance.
  • A water bottle (empty for security, then fill it up), and some snacks you like.
  • Mints or gum, which help with ear pressure and give you something to do.
  • A comforting object, a scarf or jumper that smells of home, or a book you love.
  • A short note to yourself, written now, reminding you why you're making this trip.

If you're thinking about medication for anxiety, speak to your doctor this week, not on the day. Don't try anything new for the first time on the flight.

The day before: rest and set expectations

  • Check the turbulence forecast once, if knowing helps you. Make a plan for any bumpy part, then leave it.
  • Go easy on caffeine and alcohol. Both can make anxiety feel stronger.
  • Do something kind and ordinary in the evening, like a walk, a film or a meal with someone.
  • Lay out what you need so the morning is simple.

If you can't sleep well, don't panic about it. One rough night won't stop you from getting through the day.

On the day

  • Eat something. Low blood sugar can feel a lot like anxiety.
  • Leave plenty of time and give yourself permission to sit quietly at the gate.
  • Tell the crew when you board. A quick "I'm a nervous flyer" is enough. Many crew will check in on you during the flight.
  • Buckle up and breathe. Start your slow breathing before take-off, not after the worry arrives.

Whatever happens, remember that anxiety and danger are not the same thing. You can feel scared and still be perfectly safe. And every flight you take is proof you can do it again.

Common questions

How can I calm my nerves before a flight?

Learn what is normal on a flight, practise slow breathing each day in the week before, sort out the practical details early and arrive at the airport with plenty of time. Familiar calming skills work better when you need them.

Should I drink alcohol to calm my nerves on a flight?

It is best to go easy. Alcohol may seem to help at first, but it can make anxiety feel stronger later and leave you dehydrated.

Does it help to tell the flight attendants I am nervous?

Yes. A quick "I am a nervous flyer" when you board is enough. Crew are trained to help and many will check in on you during the flight.

When should I talk to my doctor about fear of flying?

If you are thinking about medication or your fear is very strong, talk to your doctor well before your trip. Fear of flying is very treatable, and a therapist can also help.
