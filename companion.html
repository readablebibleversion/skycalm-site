How to help a nervous flyer

A two-minute read for the person sitting next to them.

Someone who trusts you has shared this because flying makes them anxious. Thank you for being there. You don’t need to fix anything. Being calm and close is most of what helps.

Before the flight

  • Ask what helps them. They may have sent you a short list with this link. If not, just ask: “What would help if you feel wobbly?”
  • Keep the day unhurried. Arriving early takes a lot of pressure off.
  • Offer them the seat they prefer. Some people feel better on the aisle, others by the window, and seats over the wing feel the fewest bumps.

If they start to panic

  • Stay calm and stay close. Your steady voice and breathing are contagious, in a good way.
  • Breathe with them. Slowly in through the nose for 4, out through the mouth for 6. Counting out loud helps.
  • Name it gently. “This is anxiety. It’s uncomfortable, and it will pass. I’m right here.” Panic usually peaks within minutes and then fades.
  • Ground them. Ask them to press their feet into the floor, or tell you three things they can see.
  • Distract if they like it. Talk about plans for the trip, a show, anything ordinary.
  • Remind them bumps and noises are normal. Turbulence is like bumps on a road, and the crew deal with it every day.

Try to avoid “just relax”, “don’t be silly”, or jokes about crashing. Even well-meant, they make it harder. Kindness and patience work far better.

If they use SkyCalm

The SOS button guides them through slow breathing with a calm voice and gentle taps, and there’s a card they can show the crew. You can simply sit with them while they use it.

When to ask the crew

Flight attendants are trained to help nervous flyers and are usually glad to. If you’re ever worried about someone’s health, tell a flight attendant straight away.

After landing

Tell them they did well. Every flight they take makes the next one a little easier.
